The problem with how artists get shown now

Most venues tell people that there is music, but they do not tell them enough about the person actually playing.

No real artist identity

A restaurant might post “Live music tonight: Jordan Miller,” but the guest still does not know what kind of music that is, whether they want to stay, or where to learn more.

Same info gets rebuilt over and over

Artists and venues keep repeating the same profile details, schedule notes, and link info manually instead of using one live source of truth.

How it works

The artist page becomes the live source.

The artist updates their own profile, schedule, links, and venue dates once. Venues can then pull that live information into their lineup cards, restaurant pages, QR menus, and music discovery views.

✓Artist controls their own bio, genre, links, and schedule
✓Venue pages can show clickable artist cards
✓Guests can tap the artist from the restaurant QR menu
✓One update can feed multiple venues and listings

What the artist page can include

This is more than a schedule page. It can be a live mini hub for the artist.

✓Profile photo and bio
✓Genre / music style tags
✓Upcoming schedule and venue list
✓Instagram, Spotify, YouTube, website links
✓Song requests, tips, and booking form

This makes the venue pages smarter too.

When a guest taps the artist from a restaurant’s QR menu or lineup card, they land on the artist’s live page. That helps the venue promote live music better, helps the artist get more exposure, and makes the whole experience feel more real and up to date.
